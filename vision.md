# Vision

## A note before we begin

**There is no right answer here, and we don't know what the right answer is.**

What follows are early thoughts, not conclusions. AI is changing what is possible in education, and nobody yet knows what the best way to use it looks like. This document describes directions worth exploring. It will change as we try things, learn, and change our minds.

---

## What does "the new era of education" mean?

The core idea: every learner and every teacher could have access to an exceptionally good explanation of any subject, at any time. Each word is carefully chosen, ideas are made simple, and visuals such as charts and diagrams are used where they help.

OLAM stands for **Open Learning Adaptive Materials**, and the two key words matter here:

- **Open:** the materials are open to use, to edit, and to build on. A teacher can use them as they are or change them to fit her class.
- **Adaptive:** the materials adapt to the learner. A student can go deeper, go slower, or go further, depending on what they need.

---

## Two different paths

OLAM looks at two different situations. They share the same ideas but serve different people:

1. **The teacher-led classroom:** a teacher in front of a class, supported by AI.
2. **The independent learner:** a student learning alone, with AI as the teacher.

---

## Path 1: The teacher-led classroom

Let's explore this vision through the eyes of a teacher. We follow her through the lesson, step by step, and describe what each step could feel like with OLAM.

### Before the lesson: preparation

It's Sunday evening, and she has a lesson on financial education on Tuesday. She opens OLAM and finds the lesson already waiting for her. It isn't just one file. It is a complete package: a few short videos, a slide deck, PDFs for the students, links to further content, and optional homework. Everything fits together, and every word was chosen with care.

She never starts from scratch. She starts from a good base, and the base is easy to edit. The second minute of a video isn't relevant for her class, so she removes it in a few clicks. A section doesn't fit her students, so she deletes it. When she wants to add something of her own, such as a story, a local example, or a question she likes to ask, that is simple too. She can even generate a new slide deck or a short video for it.

The videos are a suggestion, not a requirement. If she can't show them in her classroom for technical reasons, or she feels this isn't the right approach for her class, she can watch them herself and use them as preparation.

The package also prepares her as a teacher. Even a good teacher can use a refresher on the subject, so it includes a brief for her:

- What exactly do we mean by this topic?
- What is the flow of the class?
- Which guiding questions are worth asking the students?
- Which examples work well, and which more advanced examples might she want to add? These are optional, and she decides whether to use them.
- Which parts might be controversial or open a heated discussion?
- Which parts might be sensitive for some students?

She walks into the classroom knowing where the lesson is going and where to be careful.

#### What changes

Teachers already know how to prepare a lesson, and many do it very well. The difference we are aiming for is not in the teacher's skill, but in where her time goes. Instead of searching, adapting, and building from the beginning, she starts from something good and shapes it to her class. Instead of preparing alone, she gets support on the subject itself. The preparation becomes simpler and of higher quality at the same time.

### During the lesson: the class itself

The class is 45 minutes, and the teacher leads it from beginning to end. She controls everything: the pace, the order, what to show, and what to skip.

If possible, the lesson starts with a video. A short video, a few minutes long, opens the subject with a clear explanation backed by charts and motion visualization. Then she takes over. She asks the guiding questions, brings the examples, listens to the students, and lets the discussion go where it needs to go. A few minutes later, another short video helps explain the next idea, and she continues from there.

Altogether, the videos take about 5 to 7 minutes of a 45-minute lesson, in segments of 3 to 4 minutes, never in one long stretch. Most of the lesson belongs to the teacher and the students.

#### What changes

Explaining a subject clearly, with charts and visuals, takes a lot of preparation and energy, and it is hard to do well every time. With OLAM, that technical explanation is handled by someone else, in a way that is carefully built and keeps the students focused. The teacher saves her energy and voice for the rest of the lesson: the conversation, the questions, and the moments that need a human being. She is still the one leading the class. She just doesn't have to carry every explanation alone.

### After the lesson: homework and going deeper

The package includes homework for the students. It is optional, and the teacher decides whether to assign it, change it, or leave it out, just like everything else in the package.

Some students will find the topic interesting and want to know more. For them, the materials offer a way to dive deeper: more to read and more to explore. To keep this safe and trustworthy, the extra content comes only from sources we have validated and approved. Instead of sending students to the open web, we offer a curated set of authorized links: "If you want to learn more, choose one of these."

#### What changes

Homework is not new, and teachers already know how to assign it. What we want is for good homework to be part of the package from the start, so the teacher doesn't have to build it herself. And for the curious student, the next step is no longer a random search, but a short list of content we trust.

### Wrapping up Path 1

This is the first path: a lesson that looks very much like the one we know today. The teacher still stands in front of the class and leads it. What changes is the support around her: a strong starting point for preparation, a clear explanation delivered by someone else, and a package that includes everything from the first video to the homework.

But some students will want more than one lesson gives them. They are curious, they want to go deeper, or they want to learn at their own pace, on their own time. That brings us to the second path.

## Path 2: The independent learner

Now let's look at the same vision through the eyes of a student who wants to learn on their own, with no classroom and no schedule.

### Learning at your own pace

It's an evening, and a student wants to understand how interest works. Nobody assigned it. She is simply curious. She opens OLAM, and the best teacher in the world is there to explain it to her. The explanation is clear, simple, and supported by charts and visuals, and it is built to keep her interested.

She moves at her own pace. The first idea is easy, so she moves on quickly. The second one is harder, so she listens to it again. And again, if she needs to. No one is waiting for her, and there is nothing to be embarrassed about.

When an idea doesn't click, she asks for more examples, and the AI gives her more, until it makes sense. When she wants to go further, she can dive deeper, using content we have validated and approved. If she wants to learn more, she chooses one of the authorized links.

And when she wants to know whether she really understood, she can take an exam or a self-evaluation. It is entirely up to her. Nothing is mandatory, and no one is grading her.

#### What changes

Students who learn on their own today are often brave. They piece things together from many sources: a video here, an article there, and no easy way to know what is reliable, what is accurate, or what to learn next. The explanations vary in quality, and the pace is set by whatever happens to be available.

The vision here is to give the independent learner the same quality of explanation a good classroom aims for, in a form that fits one person: patient, repeatable, adaptive, and trustworthy, with the choice of where to go next always staying with the student.
