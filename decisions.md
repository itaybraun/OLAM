# Decisions

A log of the decisions made in the OLAM project. Each decision has a title and a body.

---

## 1. Content language: Hebrew first, English as fallback

Learning content should be written in Hebrew whenever possible.

If Hebrew causes too many problems with the tools (rendering, formatting, conversion, or AI output quality), the content will be written in English instead. This will be evaluated as the work progresses.

Project management material, including this file, the README, and other documentation about the project itself, will be written in English.

## 2. One subject only, driven by real-world value

OLAM will focus on a single subject, and only one. The tools and processes will be built around that subject and judged by the concrete, real-world value they deliver to learners, rather than built in the abstract to cover everything.

## 3. Choose a non-core subject

The subject will not be a core study such as math or English. It should be something more advanced, such as humanities. Candidates include history, archaeology, financial education, and critical thinking.

## 4. Starting subject: financial education (tentative)

The leading candidate is financial education. It is interesting, relevant well beyond Israel, and has plenty of topics to explore.

This is a starting point, not a commitment. The choice may be revisited later, and any change will be recorded as a new decision.

## 5. Avoid controversial subjects

OLAM will avoid controversial subjects. We don't know who will join the project, and we aim to reach a large audience, so the content should stay welcoming and broadly acceptable to as many people as possible.
