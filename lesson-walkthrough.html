01 Sunday

Before the lesson · Open the package

Everything for the lesson is waiting in one place.

On Sunday evening, she chooses “Compound Interest: Money That Grows on Money.” Instead of starting from a blank page, she sees a complete set of materials that fit together.

What she gets

A connected lesson kit: explanations for students, tools for class, and a briefing for the teacher.

Video 01 3 minutes

What is interest, and why does it snowball?

A friendly animated explanation starts with a question: if you lend a friend 100 shekels, what is a fair thank-you? It introduces interest, compares simple and compound interest, and shows savings growing in a clear visual example.

Video 02 3 minutes

Why time is the secret ingredient

A visual story compares two friends who save the same amount, but start at different times: the early starter ends up with more, despite saving for fewer years. It introduces the Rule of 72 and ends with an optional, more advanced look at continuous compounding and the number e.

Inside the teacher briefing

Ready before she walks into class

✳

The core idea

Compound interest means interest is calculated on the original amount and on interest already earned: money grows on top of money.

The briefing also gives the lesson flow, useful examples, optional advanced material, and points to handle with care.

Suggested 45-minute lesson

  1. 0–5 minWarm-up question
  2. 5–8 minVideo 1
  3. 8–20 minDiscussion, simple vs. compound, and a worked example
  4. 20–23 minVideo 2
  5. 23–38 minThe two-friends story, Rule of 72, and practice problems
  6. 38–43 minSummary
  7. 43–45 minOptional homework and closing

Guiding questions

“If someone gave you 100 shekels today, or 110 shekels in a year, which would you choose, and why?”
“Why do you think the line for compound interest curves upward instead of staying straight?”
“Who ends up with more: someone saving for 10 years starting early, or 20 years starting late? Guess before we look.”
“Can compound interest ever work against you? Where?”
“If you could tell your future self one thing about money, what would it be?”

Examples and care notes

  • Examples: 1,000 shekels at 5% year by year; a savings account growing over time.
  • Optional depth: the formula, continuous compounding and e, or how inflation changes the real value of interest.
Handle with care: avoid asking about students’ family savings. Debt can feel personal, so discuss it sensitively. If conversation turns to specific investments or earnings, steer back to the learning idea.

03 Tuesday

In the classroom · The teacher leads

The explanations support the class. They don’t take it over.

She controls the pace and decides what to show. The short videos open up ideas; the conversation, examples, and practice belong to her and the students.

0–5 min

Start with a question

She asks whether students would choose 100 shekels today or 110 in a year. Hands go up; she lets the debate run before revealing an answer.

5–8 min

Watch, then wonder Video 1 · 3 min

The animation introduces interest and the snowball effect. Then she takes over with the chart: “Why do you think the line curves?” Students guess before she explains.

8–20 min

Work it out together

Class works through 1,000 shekels at 5%, year by year. Students do the arithmetic and notice the pattern. She adds her own grandmother’s savings story.

20–22 min

Return to the visual Edited Video 2 · 2 min

The shortened clip shows the two-friends story and Rule of 72, then ends before the advanced formula.

22–38 min

Discuss, guess, and practice

Students compare the early and late saver, then practice the Rule of 72 in pairs. A question about loans comes up; she handles it carefully using the briefing’s heads-up.

38–45 min

Close with room for choice

She summarizes three things to remember and offers optional homework to students who want it. The class ends with a few minutes for questions.

“Most of the lesson was hers. The explaining that needed charts and animation was handled by the videos; she spent her energy on the conversation.”

04 The point

What the journey makes possible

A useful starting point, with the teacher still in charge.

She did not search for every resource or build the lesson from scratch. She arrived prepared, changed what did not fit, and stayed in control of the class from beginning to end.

Before

One package brings the teaching materials and teacher preparation together.

Adapt

She can remove one part, keep advanced material optional, and add her own story.

During

Only five minutes are video; most of the class is teacher-led learning.
